# Second Home: warm editorial redesign

## Intent
Give the existing working discovery app an authored Mid-Century Modern identity. User selected warm editorial over a playful Palm Springs treatment.

## Visual thesis
An independent travel journal printed on warm ivory paper: generous typography, walnut ink, burnt-orange accents, and a single geometric city illustration inspired by vintage travel posters.

## Content and composition
- Make Second Home the unmistakable masthead; retain the concise promise and direct discovery action.
- Replace the delicate outlined city sketch with a bold, flat geometric streetscape in a unified full-width poster composition. Use vector artwork because geometry and print-like shapes define this direction.
- Present city selection and favourites as a typeset worksheet with numbered steps and fine rules, rather than a floating rounded panel.
- Style real discoveries as editorial place entries with large photographs, prominent names, restrained evidence notes, and clear map/replacement actions.
- Reduce decorative labels, tiny badges, and repeated marketing copy. Keep unknown-data messages and meaningful explanations.

## Typography and colour
Two typefaces maximum: expressive editorial serif and a readable sans serif. Warm ivory background, dark walnut text, burnt orange for primary actions; muted olive may appear only within the illustration. Readable controls and supporting text, generous spacing, square or subtly rounded edges, minimal shadows.

## Interaction thesis
One brief masthead/illustration entrance sequence, a restrained section reveal as it enters view, and tactile button/link hover feedback. Respect reduced-motion settings. Keep input readiness safeguards and the complete city/taste/discovery/replacement workflow.

## Boundaries
Visual redesign only: retain Qloo contracts, secret handling, request limits, evidence honesty, and existing features. No new accounts, providers, or paid services. Preserve mobile usability, keyboard navigation, and clear focus states.

## Approaches
Warm editorial was selected for its human travel-journal character. Palm Springs was declined because its brighter palette and playful geometry would make the experience more recreational than reflective.

## Validation and delivery
Run the existing tests and production build, inspect desktop/mobile layouts, verify the working discovery flow, then update the existing public Site and GitHub repository. Deployment audience remains public as already authorized.

## Approval
User approved this design on October 28, 2026.
